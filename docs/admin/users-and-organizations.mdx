Organizations isolate sessions, evaluations, audits, insights, alerts, queries, dashboards, users, and keys. Confirm the active organization before changing administrative resources.

## Manage members and organizations

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Dashboard">
    1. Use the organization switcher at the top of the Cloud sidebar to change organizations.
    2. Go to **settings → members** to search members by email or filter by role.
    3. Select **+ add member**, enter the email, choose a role, and adjust single permissions if needed.
    4. Open a member later to change their role and permissions, or to remove them from the organization.

    ![The Users page showing member email addresses, permission sets, and edit and disable controls.](/images/dashboard/users.png)
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="CLI">
    ```bash
    fp orgs list
    fp orgs switch reliability-team
    fp orgs current
    fp orgs perms

    fp users create engineer@example.com --permission-set standard
    fp users show engineer@example.com
    fp users update engineer@example.com --add audits:write
    fp users disable engineer@example.com
    ```
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Administrators can add members, update their permissions, and remove them, then assign the permission set appropriate for their role. Removing a member (`fp users disable` in the CLI, a delete operation in the API) deletes only their membership in this organization.

<Warning>
  Removing a member takes them out of this organization only: their account, their sign-in, and every other organization they belong to are untouched. To bring them back, invite them again. The organization's owner and protected members cannot be removed here.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  Give service accounts descriptive names tied to a workload and owner. Avoid sharing keys between organizations or between people and machines.
</Tip>
