Your agents' code already says what they must never do: a prompt that says "never force-push to main", a tool that deletes files, an approval step before an email goes out. Once you connect GitHub, Failproof AI reads those rules, writes a policy for each, tests it, and puts it in front of you. Nothing runs until you say yes.

## How it works

1. **Connect GitHub** under **Settings › Integrations › GitHub** and select the repositories your agents live in.
2. Failproof AI links each agent to the folder that runs it and writes the agent's profile: what it is for, what it must and must not do, and which of its tools can't be undone. Each rule points at the line of code it came from.
3. For the rules a policy can enforce — "must not", "needs approval", "limit", and tools that can't be undone — it writes a policy and tests it on example calls it must stop and calls it must allow. When the agent already has traffic, the policy is also replayed against its recent calls.
4. Only policies that pass their tests are suggested, at most three per agent.

Suggestions are refreshed when the agent's code changes.

## Where you see them

- **Home › needs you** shows the best suggestion, one at a time. Choose **review** to open it, or say no in place.
- **Policies › suggested from your code** lists them all. Each card shows the rule, the line of code it came from, how it was tested, and the policy itself.

Every card says how much testing stands behind it, in plain numbers: for example *tested on 9 examples (4 to stop, 5 to allow) · no traffic yet, tested on examples only*, or *replayed on 1,240 real calls from 7 days: would have acted 3×*.

## Saying yes

**Yes, deploy** opens a short sheet:

- The machines that run this agent's kind of agent (Claude Code, Codex, …) are already selected. Uncheck any you don't want.
- The policy is deployed in **observe** mode: it runs on every call but only records what it would have stopped, and blocks nothing.
- With no machines connected yet, **publish only** keeps it ready to deploy later from its page.

Once an observed policy has acted on live traffic without stopping a working call, it shows as **ready to enforce** on Home and on the policies list. Switching it to enforce is a separate, deliberate step. See [deploy a policy](/policies/deploy).

## Saying no

**No** dismisses the suggestion. It stays dismissed until the code it came from changes.

## Who can do what

| Action | Permission |
| --- | --- |
| See suggestions | `policies:read` |
| Say yes or no | `policies:write` |

Disconnecting GitHub removes every suggestion that quotes your code. Policies you already published stay.
